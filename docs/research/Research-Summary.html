Research summary

snakewarz · three closed agendas · twenty-three phases · 2026-07-28 → 2026-08-01

Three research agendas are closed. The first built a frontier of experimental bots, the second measured it at an equal wall clock, and the third rebuilt the single-player campaign around walls, complete openings and browser cost. This page is the account: what each phase attempted, what it found, and where the ladder and Gauntlet stand now.

Every Elo figure here is quoted beside the field it was fitted in. Ratings from different fields are not comparable — these match-ups are intransitive, and a single Elo fitted over a cycle is field-composition-dependent. Where this page draws a line across time, it does so inside one field, and says so.

The ladder today

Nine shipped rungs, one field, one fit.

The span from random to alphabeta is 1,242 Elo, and the two rungs at the top are new. Both were registered experimental from the day each arrived and graduated together on 2026-07-29 — one decision, not two, because the evidence that admits puct admits alphabeta above it on stronger evidence.

The retired rung is worth reading. alphabeta:eval=chamber rates 497 against the shipped territory leaf’s 527 — and it wins the direct head-to-head 225–175, which rate flags in its own output rather than smoothing away. That is this repository’s sharpest standing rule firing on cue: a head-to-head between two settings of one bot measures a style match-up, and only a common field converts it into strength. Both rungs ran here at budget 1000, so the chamber leaf — 3.48× puct’s wall clock against territory’s 0.95× — was handed roughly three and a half times the milliseconds and still rates below. The independent equal-clock measurement that authorised the change put the same gap at +46.

The frontier over time

What was certified, against what had been measured.

The interesting thing about the two agendas is the gap between these two lines. The 2026-07-28 agenda produced eight landed phases and moved no default and no ladder rung — deliberately, because moving one moves a golden hash and a ladder threshold, which is a release decision rather than a measurement. So for a full agenda the strongest bot in the tree sat above the strongest bot a player could pick, and the whole of that gap was banked at once by the second agenda's third phase.

One caveat the chart cannot carry: strongest in the tree was not a settled question at the first agenda’s close. Its alpha-beta phase found a genuine intransitive cycle — alphabeta beat puct:eval=chamber head-to-head 108–92 while rating 62 below it in a broad field and 48 above it in a narrow one, on disjoint intervals both times, with the pairing itself stable. The company moved, not the match-up. So the dashed line is alphabeta specifically — the bot that went on to ship — and not a claim about a maximum.

Agenda 2026-07-28 — building the frontier

Ten workstreams, ranked into eight phases. Closed 2026-07-28.

The question it opened on: the ladder was settled, so does search beat reaction was no longer open. What kind of search, what kind of evaluation, and whether learned models, exact search or bitboards buy anything — those were.

PhaseAttemptedWhat came backVerdict
P1 Retraction-aware survival horizon — “moves until trapped, given my length and growth phase” in place of “cells a self-avoiding walk can take” Oracle-verified against brute force on 3×3–4×5 regions, and four of its own design bullets were wrong. The corrected leaf loses to survival by 185 Elo at equal clock. It dies earlier and in a more open board — the predicted signature, inverted. negative
P2 MCTS-Solver — propagate proven win/loss up the tree as exact rather than averaging it into a mean Correct, and free (2166 vs 2181 µs/turn). The field is monotone in how much AMAF is believed and the best of it is the control. The portfolio shipped as :lab phases, a diagnostic, and bounds itself at ~25 Elo. null

What it discovered that outlived its own phases

  • A truer number is not a better ranking function. P1’s estimator was right about one region’s physics and wrong about the ordering of two. The same bet — that FillableSpace’s parity premise being false under the shipped rules must be worth something — was then lost a second time by SPSA search in P5, which found relaxing the parity cap worth nothing.
  • A head-to-head between two settings of one bot measures a style match-up. Only a common field converts that into strength. P5’s ablation run as ab came back intransitive and put the wrong sign on the coordinate carrying the point.
  • Divergence measures whether a test can see a change, never whether the change is good. P2’s divergence table predicted where the knob changed more decisions and got the sign exactly backwards.
  • Absolute microseconds from a single timing block are worthless. The conversion agent’s first unpaired run put a control at 0.82×; the machine had degraded ~50% across the session. Only paired ratios with a control survive.
  • Build the new thing so its defaults reproduce the old thing exactly. ChamberEval at neutral weights is bit-identical to SurvivalEval, so every later batch between them is a batch about three weights and nothing else.

Agenda 2026-07-29 — measuring it, and shipping

Eight workstreams over eight phases. Closed 2026-07-30.

Its stated goal was to establish the defaults the repository ships, measure the configuration nobody had measured, and turn the previous agenda’s findings into something a player actually gets. It did all three, and the answer to the second was not the one the agenda was written expecting.

What it discovered that outlived its own phases

  • Equal allowance is not equal milliseconds, and the gap is 4.7×. Because a rollout costs a flat 1 against the budget, a dearer policy buys no fewer iterations and its whole cost lands on the wall clock — so an equal-allowance batch flatters the candidate. Every published comparison between leaves had given the dearer one several times the clock.
  • Equal train and holdout loss is not a statement about transfer. Six phases read one number as “the ceiling is the features”. It says the fit is not short of capacity on the population it was fitted to, and nothing whatever about another one.
  • A hot-path rewrite is settled in the browser. :bots deploys to wasm and nowhere else, and P8 showed the JVM cannot be trusted for the sign. Now part of SW-03.
  • Multiply by steps per rollout before comparing a rate to a threshold. P5’s step-0 kill criterion used the per-step denominator and would have killed the one candidate worth keeping.
  • Two phases in a row found that a mechanism named in the agenda was not the mechanism in the code. Both were caught by instrumenting the claim before building on it.

The equal-clock reckoning

The composed field: twelve rungs, 13,200 matches, one fit. 12×12, build ea069e8.

This is the instrument the first agenda’s three shipping decisions were waiting on, and the reason none of them was taken. Every candidate had been measured alone, against a different baseline, in a different field, at an allowance that gave the dearer leaf several times the clock. Seated together and handed matched wall clocks, the picture inverts.

Three of the four rows are the same finding twice over. The dearer leaves were not measured wrong; they were measured against a question a shipping decision does not ask. eval=chamber costs 3.45× puct’s wall clock at the shipped allowance, so at equal clock it gets roughly a quarter of the evaluations — and a quarter of the evaluations is worth more than a better leaf. Agenda 2026-08-01 — rebuilding the Gauntlet

Seven phases, six map-separated baselines, a seven-level campaign, and one failed human ramp.

The third agenda treated every wall layout as a different game. It added the complete empty-8 opening population (40 oriented starts, both seatings), fixed Chrome effort lanes, and six separate baseline fields. Its policy work shipped cartographer; its fixed mini-search shipped lookahead. A learned action policy failed its held-out agreement gate, and the exact solver stopped honestly at its 728 MiB feasibility bound after finding no correction to make through the largest complete tier.

OutcomeEvidence retained at the close
Complete boss measurement Territory alpha-beta@1700 won the deployable empty-8 common field, directly cleared both chamber incumbents, fit the 10.5 ms boss lane, and is described as strongest measured rather than optimal.
Machine-qualified campaign UCT@100 scored 69/66/61/63/25/25/11%; PUCT@250 scored 98/99/95/82/55/45/17%, with no adjacent rise above five points and no forfeits.
Human gate failed One roughly five-minute session cleared levels 1 through 6. Level 7 alone supplied the intended challenge, so machine monotonicity did not establish human difficulty.

The shipped seven levels preserve replay compatibility and use a fresh snakewarz.gauntlet.v2 campaign identity. The follow-up agenda keeps levels 1, 2 and 7, and reopens only levels 3 through 6 on their exact maps.

What the first two agendas produced besides bots

Across sixteen phases, one added a bot and one moved a shipped default. What the other fourteen shipped is measurement apparatus, evaluation values, and knobs that are off until somebody types them — and on the evidence of the second agenda correcting the first at four separate points, that is where most of the value went.

InstrumentWhat it exists to stop
:lab spsa with a mandatory confirming run A search shape manufacturing a confident wrong point on a flat knob. Both descent and SPSA do it; only fresh boards catch it, so there is no flag to skip the confirmation.
:lab phases and report/Separation.kt Not knowing where a bot’s Elo goes. It splits a replayed match at the move the board came apart, and produced the first map: 67% of the strongest leaf’s losses were already behind on room when the board split.
AppraisalTape A cost instrument whose reading depends on the bot being timed. The subject is seated at no slot, over a line another bot plays, with a median rather than a minimum over passes.
The distinct-games line, printed by every batch A hundred matches that are four games played twenty-five times. Nothing in a win matrix says so.
Per-entrant budget= and the measured allowance table Comparing two leaves at an allowance that hands the dearer one four times the clock.
The cross-target golden set, re-run in real Chrome A bot whose move stream diverges between the JVM test target and the browser that ships. Every registry entry now has a case.
A contract suite sweeping 1–4 seats and every Choice value A bot written as a duel. A 2,160-match probe found none — every search here was already seat-count general — but nothing had pinned it.

Open

The largest items either agenda left for a person, in the order someone would pick them up.

  • freeSquaresOf is 13–14% of every ownership sweep and is a per-cell loop, so nothing above it can reach it. It wants a word-level free mask in :core’s Occupancy. That is not a fusion, so P8’s negative says nothing about it — and it is the largest speed item anybody has a number for.
  • Board-size-conditioned defaults. Two independent arguments now, the second with a number: uct:rolloutDepth=25 is worth +78 at 20×20, +13 at 12×12 and nothing at all at 8×8 — level with the baseline at equal allowance, so there is nothing there for a cheaper allowance to rescue. A single default cannot be right, and the contract has no way to express two.
  • SequentialTest is two-seat only, which is what keeps three-seat work from deciding anything: ab, tune and spsa were all unavailable for the whole of P7, so every number it produced is a field rating. Making it seat-count aware turns three-seat measurement from mapping into deciding.
  • Whether Kotlin/Wasm’s array bounds handling is what reversed P8’s sign is unverified. The evidence is a scaling law plus a control that moved as predicted, not a disassembly. Somebody who wants to write fast wasm here would learn more from settling that than from anything else on this list.
  • The reference machine. Absolute Chrome milliseconds carry a 4.1× scaling factor (range 3.4–5.0) between the measuring box and the machine the published tables were taken on. One browser run there replaces the scaling with a measurement. Ratios and the allowance table are unaffected.
